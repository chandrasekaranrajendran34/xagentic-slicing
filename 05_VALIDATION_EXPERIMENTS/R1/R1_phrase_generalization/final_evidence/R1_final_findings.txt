R1 — Phrase Generalization and Lexical Ablation
================================================

Experimental design
-------------------
R1 was evaluated across five random seeds:
42, 43, 44, 45, and 46.

Four conditions were evaluated:

L1 — Original:
Original synthetic intent text using the frozen feature pipeline.

L2 — Phrase-family-held-out:
Strict class-specific phrase/use-case family holdout.
Train and test phrase families are disjoint.
Generic use cases are excluded from this condition.

L3 — Generic + KPI:
Use-case semantic phrases are replaced with a neutral
"network service" expression while preserving observable KPI
information.

L4 — KPI-only:
Only KPI information observable to the frozen parser is retained.

Final five-seed results
-----------------------
L1 accuracy:
0.9311 ± 0.0065

L1 macro-F1:
0.9311 ± 0.0065

L2 accuracy:
0.9549 ± 0.0081

L2 macro-F1:
0.9548 ± 0.0081

L3 accuracy:
0.8920 ± 0.0080

L3 macro-F1:
0.8920 ± 0.0078

L4 accuracy:
0.8920 ± 0.0080

L4 macro-F1:
0.8920 ± 0.0078

Lexical-ablation effect
-----------------------
Mean L1 -> L3/L4 accuracy reduction:
0.0391 ±
0.0090

Mean L1 -> L3/L4 macro-F1 reduction:
0.0392 ±
0.0089

Interpretation
--------------
1. Performance remains strong under strict held-out
   class-specific phrase families.

2. Therefore, the classifier does not exhibit performance
   collapse when evaluated on phrase families excluded from
   training.

3. Removing lexical/use-case information produces a consistent
   performance reduction across all five seeds.

4. Consequently, lexical intent cues provide useful predictive
   information, but classification is not solely dependent on
   lexical phrase memorization.

5. L2 must NOT be described as an improvement over L1.
   L2 uses a different evaluation population because generic
   examples are excluded.

6. L3 and L4 produce identical frozen feature representations
   across all five evaluated corpora. Their identical results are
   therefore an architectural/representation finding rather than
   independent evidence from two distinct model representations.

7. Ambiguous intents require separate interpretation because the
   synthetic ground-truth label is defined before KPI blending.
   KPI-only disagreement on such cases should not automatically be
   interpreted as ordinary classification failure.

Conclusion
----------
R1 provides evidence against the concern that the reported
classifier performance is explained solely by memorization of
class-specific intent phrases.

The experiment also quantifies the contribution of lexical cues:
removing those cues reduces macro-F1 by approximately
3.92 percentage points on average.

Claims should remain limited to the evaluated synthetic corpus,
frozen parser, feature representation, model-selection procedure,
and phrase families used in this experiment.
